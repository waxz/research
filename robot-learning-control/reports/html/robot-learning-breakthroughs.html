Robot learning breakthroughs, 2010–2026

One breakthrough per year, chosen for how much it changed what robots could do next. Each entry gives the bottleneck it inherited, the experiment that proved it worked, and where it reached real machines. Read in order, each year answers the limitation exposed by the one before.

Model-based control held the hardware until about 2015. Simulation-trained RL took over locomotion and dexterity from 2017 to 2022. Imitation and reference motion took over manipulation and humanoids from 2023. 2026 joins RL and reference motion through a learned model.

Year by year

2010

Apprenticeship learning flies helicopter aerobatics REF

Bottleneck inheritedHand-designing controllers for maneuvers at the edge of the flight envelope was infeasible.
BreakthroughLearn the target trajectory and a dynamics model from several expert demonstrations, then control with model-based optimal control.
EvidenceAbbeel, Coates & Ng (IJRR 2010): autonomous aerobatics such as flips, rolls, tic-tocs and chaos, at expert-pilot level and in some cases better.
Real-world reachEstablished learning from demonstration for agile vehicles; the demo-plus-model pattern returns in 2023–26.
Also: Ross & Bagnell prove that behavior cloning's error compounds with the square of the horizon, which motivates DAgger in 2011.
2011

PILCO: data-efficient learning with a probabilistic model MB

Bottleneck inheritedModel-free RL needed far too many real trials; learned models introduced bias that broke policies.
BreakthroughA Gaussian-process dynamics model that tracks its own uncertainty, with analytic policy gradients through it.
EvidenceDeisenroth & Rasmussen (ICML 2011): a real cart-pole learned swing-up and balance from seconds of interaction, orders of magnitude fewer trials than model-free methods.
Real-world reachSmall real systems (cart-pole, low-cost arms). GPs didn't scale to high dimensions; neural models replaced them later.
Also: DAgger (Ross, Gordon & Bagnell) reduces imitation error growth to linear by querying the expert on the learner's own states. It became the basis of every later teacher–student pipeline.
2012

MuJoCo and online trajectory optimization MB

Bottleneck inheritedSimulators were too slow and contact-inaccurate for optimization or learning loops.
BreakthroughMuJoCo (Todorov, Erez & Tassa, IROS 2012): fast, stable contact dynamics designed for control. The same group ran MPC on full humanoid models in simulation.
EvidenceTassa et al. (IROS 2012) synthesized getting up, balancing and walking for a simulated humanoid by trajectory optimization in near real time.
Real-world reachMuJoCo became the standard RL benchmark engine. Its GPU successor MuJoCo Warp trains SuperMimic in 2026.
Also: AlexNet's ImageNet win makes deep networks a viable function approximator for perception and, soon, control.
2013

Deep Q-Network learns control from pixels RL

Bottleneck inheritedRL relied on hand-crafted state features and couldn't use raw sensors.
BreakthroughA convolutional Q-network trained end-to-end from pixels with experience replay.
EvidenceMnih et al. (arXiv 1312.5602): on seven Atari games it beat all previous approaches on six and a human expert on three, with one architecture.
Real-world reachNone directly, but it started deep RL and the investment that funded robot RL labs.
Also: Kober, Bagnell & Peters publish the standard survey of RL in robotics, showing how few real-robot successes existed before deep learning.
2014

Trajectory optimization through contact becomes a teacher MB

Bottleneck inheritedDeep RL was sample-hungry. Optimal control was efficient but produced trajectories, not reusable policies, and needed contact schedules fixed in advance.
BreakthroughContact-implicit trajectory optimization (Posa, Cantu & Tedrake, IJRR 2014) lets the optimizer choose contacts. Guided policy search (Levine & Abbeel, NeurIPS 2014) uses local trajectory optimizers to supervise a neural policy.
EvidenceGuided policy search trained neural policies for simulated swimming, hopping and walking, and for real manipulation, with far fewer samples than model-free RL.
Real-world reachSet up the 2015–16 visuomotor robot results. The optimizer-as-teacher pattern returns in DTC (2024) and in distillation pipelines.
2015

Two worlds: deep RL masters simulation; model-based humanoids struggle at the DARPA Robotics Challenge MB RL

Bottleneck inheritedDeep RL worked for discrete Atari actions, not continuous high-DoF control. Real humanoids still ran hand-built model-based stacks.
BreakthroughTRPO, GAE and DDPG bring stable deep RL to continuous control. DQN is published in Nature.
EvidenceDQN reached human-professional level on about half of 49 Atari games. TRPO/GAE learned 3-D simulated humanoid running from scratch. At the DRC Finals (June 2015), KAIST's DRC-HUBO won with model-based control, and many entrants fell.
Real-world reachThe DRC falls showed that model-based humanoids were brittle outside the conditions they were tuned for, which made simulation-trained learned controllers worth pursuing.
2016

Learning on real robots at scale: end-to-end visuomotor control and the grasping farm RL

Bottleneck inheritedPerception and control were engineered separately, and deep RL had barely touched real hardware.
BreakthroughNeural policies that map camera pixels directly to motor torques. A fleet of robots collects self-supervised grasping data.
EvidenceLevine, Finn et al. (JMLR 2016): a 92k-parameter CNN policy screwed caps onto bottles from raw images. Levine et al. 2016: over 800,000 grasp attempts over two months, on 6–14 robots at a time, produced a grasping network that grasps novel objects and corrects its own mistakes.
Real-world reachShowed robot learning is limited by data, which led to sim-to-real and later to large demonstration datasets.
Also: AlphaGo beats Lee Sedol 4–1, and Kuindersma et al. publish the model-based Atlas stack from the DRC.
2017

Domain randomization and PPO make simulation transferable RL

Bottleneck inheritedReal-world data was slow and costly to collect, and simulation-trained policies failed on hardware because of the reality gap.
BreakthroughRandomize the simulator's visuals and physics so that reality looks like one more variation. PPO gives a simple, stable, parallel optimizer.
EvidenceTobin et al. (IROS 2017): an object detector trained only on randomly textured simulated images was accurate to 1.5 cm on real images and supported grasping in clutter. Heess et al. showed rich locomotion emerging from simple rewards, though with unnatural gaits.
Real-world reachDomain randomization and PPO became the default sim-to-real recipe for the next decade.
2018

Dactyl: dexterous in-hand manipulation learned entirely in simulation RL

Bottleneck inheritedMulti-fingered manipulation (24 DoF, hidden contacts) was beyond both model-based control and real-world RL.
BreakthroughMassive distributed PPO with heavy physics and visual randomization, trained with no demonstrations.
EvidenceOpenAI (arXiv 1808.00177): a Shadow hand reoriented a block from vision, with up to 50 consecutive successes in tests. Training collected about 100 years of simulated experience in 50 hours on 6,144 CPU cores and 8 GPUs. Finger gaiting emerged without being programmed.
Real-world reachProved sim-only RL can transfer contact-rich skills, at very high compute cost.
Also: DeepMimic (reference-tracking RL for flips and kicks, in simulation), QT-Opt (96% real grasp success on unseen objects from 580k real attempts), and MIT Cheetah 3 convex MPC.
2019

Sim-to-real RL beats model-based control on a real quadruped RL

Bottleneck inheritedSim-to-real worked for arms and hands but not for dynamically balancing legged robots, where motor dynamics dominate.
BreakthroughA neural actuator model learned from real motor data and inserted into the simulator, followed by RL in simulation and zero-shot deployment.
EvidenceHwangbo et al. (Science Robotics 2019): ANYmal followed velocity commands more precisely and energy-efficiently than before, ran faster than its previous controller, and recovered from falls in complex configurations.
Real-world reachStarted the field's move from MPC to simulation-trained RL for legged robots.
Also: OpenAI solves a Rubik's cube with a robot hand using automatic domain randomization (about 60% success on moderately scrambled cubes, 20% on the hardest).
2020

Privileged teacher, proprioceptive student: zero-shot rough terrain RL

Bottleneck inherited2019 policies worked on lab floors. Natural terrain is unseen in training and unobservable without perception.
BreakthroughTrain an RL teacher with privileged terrain information, then distill it (DAgger) into a student that uses only joint and IMU history, with a terrain curriculum.
EvidenceLee et al. (Science Robotics 2020): two generations of ANYmal walked zero-shot on mud, snow, rubble, thick vegetation and flowing water, none of which appeared in training.
Real-world reachThe same ETH group and ANYmal platform were part of team CERBERUS, which won the DARPA Subterranean Challenge Final in 2021.
Also: Dreamer learns behaviors by backpropagating through a learned latent world model, the method family SuperMimic later scales.
2021

Massively parallel GPU simulation: learn to walk in minutes RL

Bottleneck inheritedEach policy took days on CPU clusters, so iteration was slow and labs needed large budgets.
BreakthroughIsaac Gym runs thousands of robots on one GPU. legged_gym adds a game-style terrain curriculum and tuned PPO.
EvidenceRudin et al. (CoRL 2022, arXiv 2021): ANYmal policies for flat terrain in under 4 minutes and for rough terrain in 20 minutes on one workstation GPU, transferred to the real robot, orders of magnitude faster than earlier work.
Real-world reachMade learned locomotion standard on Unitree, ANYmal and university robots. RMA (Kumar et al., RSS 2021) added fast online adaptation on a low-cost A1.
Also: AMP replaces hand-tuned style rewards with a motion-data discriminator, and CERBERUS wins the DARPA SubT Final.
2022

Perceptive locomotion in the wild RL

Bottleneck inheritedProprioceptive policies walked blind; they could not plan foot placement on stairs or gaps.
BreakthroughA belief encoder that fuses height-map perception with proprioception and learns when to distrust vision (tall grass, snow, reflections).
EvidenceMiki et al. (Science Robotics 2022): ANYmal hiked a 2.2 km Alpine trail on Mt. Etzel with 120 m of climb, reached the summit in 31 minutes against a 35-minute human signpost estimate, and finished the loop in 78 minutes with no falls.
Real-world reachBrought learned locomotion close to the reliability that industrial inspection quadrupeds working in plants, mines and outdoor sites need.
Also: SHAC (short-horizon analytic gradients through differentiable simulation) and DayDreamer (world models learning to walk directly on a real robot).
2023

RL beats human champions in the physical world; imitation learning wins manipulation RL REF

Bottleneck inheritedWould learned control match the best humans at a real competitive task? And how do robots get manipulation skills that have no simulator or reward?
BreakthroughSwift: RL with an empirically fitted model of the drone's perception and dynamics. Diffusion Policy and ACT: expressive generative models over action sequences, trained on teleoperated demonstrations.
EvidenceKaufmann et al. (Nature 2023): Swift won 15 of 25 races against three world-champion pilots and set the fastest lap, half a second ahead of the best human. Diffusion Policy improved on prior state of the art by 46.9% on average across 12 tasks. ACT learned six fine bimanual tasks at 80–90% success from 10 minutes of demonstrations on low-cost hardware.
Real-world reachSplit the field: RL where the task can be scored and simulated, imitation where it can be demonstrated (household, kitchen, deformables).
Limits: Swift failed under lighting conditions it wasn't trained for, where human pilots adapted.
2024

Learned humanoid control and generalist robot policies reach hardware REF RL

Bottleneck inheritedHumanoids still relied on model-based stacks; manipulation policies were single-task.
BreakthroughTransformer RL policies for humanoid walking. Mocap retargeting plus teacher–student tracking for teleoperation (H2O, OmniH2O, HumanPlus). Vision-language-action models trained on multi-robot data (π0).
EvidenceRadosavovic et al. (Science Robotics 2024): a fully learned controller walked a real humanoid over varied outdoor terrain, zero-shot from simulation, and adapted in context. π0 folded laundry, cleared tables and assembled boxes, and could be fine-tuned to new skills.
Real-world reachHumanoid companies (Unitree, Figure, Agility, Boston Dynamics' electric Atlas) moved toward learned whole-body control and demonstration-trained manipulation.
2025

General humanoid motion trackers and humanoids in public events REF

Bottleneck inheritedEach humanoid skill needed its own reward and policy, and sim-to-real broke on agile motions.
BreakthroughOne reference-tracking policy for a whole motion library (BeyondMimic), with ASAP's learned correction for the sim-to-real gap, and guided diffusion to turn tracking into tasks.
EvidenceBeyondMimic deployed diverse, highly dynamic human motions on a real Unitree G1 from one tracking recipe, and steered them to tasks with guided diffusion. At the Beijing humanoid half-marathon on 19 April 2025, Tiangong Ultra finished in 2:40:42, guided by a human pacer; the other robots took over 3 hours.
Real-world reachEntertainment, teleoperation and data collection with humanoids. Public demos showed what still needs human help (pacers, remote control, battery swaps).
Also: DreamerV3 published in Nature; RWM trains real-robot policies inside a learned neural simulator.
2026

Scaling motion tracking: SONIC at cluster scale, SuperMimic on one GPU REF RL MB

Bottleneck inheritedGeneral trackers improve with more motion data, but PPO training cost grows with the library: SONIC needed ~21,000 GPU-hours.
BreakthroughSONIC (Science Robotics) shows that more data, capacity and compute give natural general humanoid control. SuperMimic replaces PPO with backpropagation through one learned differentiable world model.
EvidenceSuperMimic (CoRL 2026, preprint): beat SONIC's return in 3 h on one H100 (>1000× fewer GPU-hours). It failed 25 of 129,514 motions versus 179 for SONIC, and 0 after 9.8 h on two GPUs. Deployed on a real G1 with flips and side rolls. Not yet independently reproduced.
Real-world reachIf it holds up, scaling-law studies of whole-body control become affordable for any lab. Flat ground only so far.

The logic of the sequence

Each step came from a specific failure of the step before it.

Hand-designed control can't do edge-of-envelope maneuvers→Learn from demonstrations (2010)
Real trials are expensive→Learn a model, optimize inside it (2011)
Learning loops need fast contact physics→MuJoCo (2012)
RL needs hand-made features→Deep networks, end-to-end (2013–15)
Model-based humanoids are brittle (DRC 2015)→Learned controllers on real robots (2016)
Real data doesn't scale→Simulation + domain randomization (2017–18)
Legged dynamics break sim-to-real→Learned actuator models (2019)
Unseen terrain, no perception→Privileged teacher → student (2020), perception fusion (2022)
Training takes days→GPU-parallel simulation, minutes (2021)
No simulator or reward for household tasks→Generative imitation, VLAs (2023–24)
RL motion looks unnatural; one policy per skill→Reference-tracking at library scale (2025–26)
Library-scale PPO costs ~21k GPU-hours→Analytic gradients through a learned world model (2026)

What the record shows

Model-based ideas never left. They moved inside learning. PILCO's learned model (2011), guided policy search's optimizer-as-teacher (2014), Hwangbo's learned actuator model (2019) and SuperMimic's world model (2026) all put a model at the center of a learning system.

Compute and data, not algorithms, set the pace from 2016 to 2023. 800k real grasps (2016), 100 simulated years for Dactyl (2018), then 20 minutes on one GPU for locomotion (2021). Each jump in data throughput opened a new class of tasks.

Specifying behavior has become the new bottleneck. Once training became cheap, the hard part was saying what to do. That pushed the field to demonstrations (2023), motion libraries (2025) and language (VLAs).

Real-world results remain narrower than demos suggest. Swift failed under unfamiliar lighting, the marathon winner needed a pacer, and SuperMimic is flat-ground only. Each year's entry has a deployment caveat.

Selection is a judgement call, made by asking which result most changed what was possible on real robots the following year. Where a year had several contenders, the others are listed under “Also”.
