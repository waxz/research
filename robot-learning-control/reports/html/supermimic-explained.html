SuperMimic, explained

On SuperMimic: Scaling Humanoid Whole-Body Control with One Differentiable World Model by Q. Liao, Y. Shao, J. Long, K. Zakka, R. Horowitz, P. Abbeel, K. Sreenath (UC Berkeley, Oct 2026), and the line of work that led to it.

SuperMimic trains a single humanoid motion tracker for an entire motion-capture library on one GPU. Instead of PPO in a simulator, it learns a differentiable neural model of the robot's dynamics and pushes exact gradients of the tracking return back through short imagined rollouts. This page walks through the math, the training loop, and the forty years of reinforcement learning that make the idea work.

1 · The problemTrack every motion in a library with one policy

The task is physics-based motion tracking. A reference clip (retargeted human mocap) says where every body of a Unitree G1 should be at each instant. The policy outputs joint set-points so that the simulated, and later the real, robot reproduces the clip without falling. Train one policy across thousands of clips and you get a general whole-body controller that other interfaces (velocity commands, VR teleoperation, text-to-motion) can drive.

The paper formalizes this as a goal-conditioned POMDP. The policy is deterministic, which matters later because there is no action log-probability to differentiate:

Policy, observation, action \[ a_t = \pi_\theta(o_t, g_t), \qquad o_t = \bar o_t + \eta_t,\;\; \eta_t \sim \rho_\eta, \qquad q^{\text{sp}} = \alpha\,a_t + q^0 \]

Here \(o_t\) is proprioception (joint positions and velocities, IMU angular velocity, projected gravity, previous action, history of 10 frames), \(g_t\) is the next \(K=10\) reference frames, and the action is a normalized PD set-point around the default pose \(q^0\). Because the PD gains are low, the set-point acts like a torque command.

Instead of hard episode ends, returns are weighted by a survival weight \(c_t\) that drops to zero after termination. In imagination the model predicts a termination probability \(\hat d_t\in[0,1]\), so the weight stays differentiable:

Objective with soft survival \[ c_0 = 1,\quad c_{t+1} = c_t\,(1-d_{t+1}), \qquad J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t\ge 0} \gamma^t\, c_t\, r_{t+1}\Big] \]

The reward is BeyondMimic's tracking reward [30]: a sum of Gaussian kernels on body positions, orientations, and velocities, plus small regularizers.

Tracking reward (tracked body set 𝒯) \[ r = \sum_{k} w_k \exp\!\Big(-\tfrac{1}{|\mathcal T|}\sum_{b\in\mathcal T} \tfrac{\|e_{k,b}\|^2}{\sigma_k^2}\Big) \;-\; 0.1\,\|a_t-a_{t-1}\|^2 \;-\; 2\sum_j \big[\max(0,q_j-q_j^{\max})+\max(0,q_j^{\min}-q_j)\big] \]
Term \(e_{k,b}\)σWeight
Body position \(p_b^{\text{des}}-p_b\)0.3 m1.0

The self-collision penalty from BeyondMimic is removed. It existed to stop a stochastic policy from pressing limbs together to suppress action noise, an exploit a deterministic policy cannot use.

2 · World modelA deterministic, differentiable stand-in for MuJoCo

The simulator knows the physics but doesn't give gradients (MuJoCo Warp contacts are non-smooth, and the code isn't written to be differentiated). SuperMimic therefore learns a surrogate. All of the simulator's randomness comes from known sources (domain-randomization context \(\xi\) and sensor noise \(\eta\)), so the model can be deterministic: it conditions on \(\xi\) and predicts the noise-free observation, and noise is added outside.

World model heads \[ \hat s_{t+1} = f_\omega(\hat s_t, a_t, \xi), \quad \hat r_{t+1} = r_\omega(\hat s_t, a_t, g_t), \quad \hat{\bar o}_t = g_\omega(\hat s_t, \xi), \quad \hat d_t = d_\omega(\hat s_t, g_t) \]

The dynamics net \(f_\omega\) is a 3 × 2048 MLP. Any head that can be computed analytically from the state (the reward, for example, is a known function of body poses) skips its loss term. The model is trained on multi-step autoregressive windows (\(k=8\)): seed it with a logged state, then feed it its own predictions along the logged actions.

Eq. 1 · World-model loss \[ \mathcal{L}_{\text{WM}}(\omega) = \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal B}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{k} \|\hat s_j - s_j\|^2 + (\hat r_j - r_j)^2 + \|\hat{\bar o}_j - \bar o_j\|^2 + \mathrm{BCE}(\hat d_j, d_j)\Big], \qquad \hat s_0 = s_0 \]

Training on its own rolled-out predictions teaches the model to correct its own drift. The authors report it stays stable well past 8 steps, which is why 32-step imagination works. The 8-step prediction error stays between 0.007 and 0.011 (normalized) as the data grows from 1 to 264 hours. Every clip is the same robot with the same contacts, so more clips mean more evidence about one shared system, not more to memorize.

The model also runs fast. On an H100 it takes more than 10× as many steps per second as MuJoCo Warp at 2k–16k parallel environments, and 62× at 64k.

3 · Policy learningBack-propagate the return through imagination

With \(\omega\) frozen, each update samples 4096 start states \(s_0\) (with their recorded \(\xi\)) from the buffer and rolls the policy forward for \(H=32\) steps inside the model. A critic \(V_\psi\) scores whatever lies beyond the horizon.

Eq. 2 · Imagined objective \[ \hat J(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s_0\sim\rho_0}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{H-1} \gamma^t \hat c_t\, \hat r_{t+1} \;+\; \gamma^H \hat c_H\, V_\psi(\hat s_H, g_H, \xi)\Big], \qquad a_t = \pi_\theta(\hat{\bar o}_t + \eta_t,\, g_t) \]

Why this gradient is better than PPO's

PPO treats the dynamics as a black box. It samples actions from a stochastic policy and estimates the gradient by the likelihood-ratio (score-function) trick:

Zeroth-order: likelihood-ratio estimator (REINFORCE / PPO) \[ \nabla_\theta J \approx \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,t} \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(a_t^{i}\mid s_t^{i})\, \hat A_t^{i} \]

This only says “that action was better than average,” so a 29-dimensional action space needs a huge number of samples to average out the noise. BPTT instead differentiates the return with respect to each action directly, using the chain rule through the model. One way to write it, with \(\lambda_t = \partial \hat J / \partial \hat s_t\) as the adjoint and the critic term at \(t=H\):

First-order: pathwise / reparameterized gradient (illustrative expansion) \[ \nabla_\theta \hat J = \sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\Big(\gamma^t \hat c_t\,\frac{\partial \hat r_{t+1}}{\partial a_t} + \lambda_{t+1}\,\frac{\partial f_\omega}{\partial a_t}\Big)\frac{\partial \pi_\theta}{\partial \theta}, \qquad \lambda_t = \gamma^t\hat c_t\frac{\partial \hat r_{t+1}}{\partial \hat s_t} + \lambda_{t+1}\Big(\frac{\partial f_\omega}{\partial \hat s_t} + \frac{\partial f_\omega}{\partial a_t}\frac{\partial \pi_\theta}{\partial o}\frac{\partial g_\omega}{\partial \hat s_t}\Big) \]

The paper writes only “BPTT through the Jacobians”. The recursion above is the standard adjoint form of that, added here to show what autodiff computes. Termination-probability terms through \(\hat c_t\) are omitted for clarity.

Two things keep this from blowing up the way gradients through a real differentiable simulator can [23]. First, the learned model is smooth, so stiff contacts don't produce exploding or misleading Jacobians. Second, the horizon is short, so products of Jacobians stay well-conditioned. The paper's ablation isolates the effect. Run PPO inside the same world model (PPO-WM), and SuperMimic still reaches PPO-WM's best return with about 64× fewer imagined steps. The speedup isn't only from fast rollouts.

The critic

The critic is fit to λ-returns computed backward over each imagined rollout, starting from its own estimate at the horizon:

Eq. 3 · Critic targets and loss \[ \hat R_t = \hat r_{t+1} + \gamma\,(1-\hat d_{t+1})\big[(1-\lambda)V_{t+1} + \lambda \hat R_{t+1}\big], \quad \hat R_H = V_H, \qquad \mathcal L_V(\psi) = \mathbb E\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{H-1}\big(V_t - \hat R_t\big)^2\Big] \]

Together these form a short-horizon actor-critic in the style of SHAC [22], with two changes: a soft survival weight instead of hard resets, and no target network. The horizon sweep on LAFAN1 shows why the critic is needed:

At equal nominal return, the critic changes behavior. Push the robot at 0.5–4 m/s while it holds a T-pose: the critic policy settles in 0.5–2.4 s, while the critic-free policy keeps stumbling and falls at 4 m/s.

Algorithm

  1. for each iteration (320k) do
  2. Roll out πθ with noise ε ~ 𝒩(0, 0.1²) in 4096 sim envs; push to 𝓑 (FIFO).
  3. Reset envs by return-based sampling: 50 % uniform over 1-s bins, 40 % softmax(−25·Gb/Gmax), 10 % historical weight.
  4. Sample 8-step windows from 𝓑; roll fω on logged actions; ω ← ω − α∇ωℒWM.
  5. Sample 4096 start states + ξ from 𝓑; imagine H = 32 steps with frozen fω.
  6. θ ← θ + α∇θĴ (Adam, β = (0.9, 0.95); clip global norm 1.0, then element-wise 0.1).
  7. Fit Vψ to λ-returns (γ = 0.99, λ = 0.95), 5 epochs × 4 minibatches.
  8. end for · update-to-data ratio 0.4 · one policy update per model update

Downstream interfaces through the same model

The policy is split into goal encoders and a shared decoder, \(z = \mathrm{enc}_{\theta,i}(g)\), \(a = \mathrm{dec}_\theta(z, o)\), with a 32-dimensional tanh-bounded latent. Any new interface \(i\) (velocity commands, end-effector targets) trains through the same frozen-physics model with the lifted objective \(\max_\theta \mathbb E_{i\sim p(\mathcal I)}[\hat J_i(\theta)]\). A velocity encoder trained on its own velocity reward (RL) beat a distillation baseline on 650 of 672 step commands, and only the distilled encoder fell, on fast turns with almost no mocap data. The authors call this preliminary.

4 · How we got hereFrom Bellman to one-GPU humanoids

SuperMimic combines two lines of research that grew up separately. One is physics-based motion imitation, mostly driven by model-free RL. The other is model-based RL with learned or differentiable dynamics. The timeline shows where each idea entered.

1957–1992

Foundations: value functions, models, policy gradients

Dynamic programming gives the Bellman optimality equation. Dyna (Sutton, 1991) already uses a learned model to generate imagined experience for the learner; SuperMimic calls its own loop “Dyna-style”. REINFORCE gives the likelihood-ratio gradient that PPO still uses.

\[ Q^*(s,a) = \mathbb E\big[r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q^*(s',a')\big], \qquad \nabla_\theta J = \mathbb E\big[\textstyle\sum_t \nabla_\theta\log\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)\,G_t\big] \]
Bellman 1957 · Sutton 1991 [1] · Williams 1992 [2] · Sutton & Barto 2018 [S1]
2011–2015

Analytic gradients through models

PILCO fits a Gaussian-process dynamics model and differentiates long-horizon expected cost analytically, which is extremely data-efficient on small systems. Stochastic Value Gradients (SVG) bring pathwise gradients through learned neural models together with a critic. Its SVG(0) variant later trains the NPMP humanoid controller. This is the direct ancestor of Eq. 2.

Deisenroth & Rasmussen 2011 [3] · Heess et al. 2015 [4]
2015–2018

Deep model-free RL

DQN and then continuous-control methods (TRPO, DDPG, GAE, PPO, SAC) scale RL with neural networks. PPO's clipped surrogate becomes the default for simulated robots because it is stable and parallelizes well:

\[ L^{\text{CLIP}}(\theta) = \mathbb E_t\Big[\min\big(\rho_t \hat A_t,\; \mathrm{clip}(\rho_t, 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat A_t\big)\Big], \quad \rho_t = \frac{\pi_\theta(a_t|s_t)}{\pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_t|s_t)} \]
Mnih 2015 [5] · Schulman 2015 TRPO [6] · Lillicrap 2016 [7] · Schulman 2016 GAE [8] · Schulman 2017 PPO [9] · Haarnoja 2018 SAC [10]
2018–2021

Motion imitation for simulated characters

DeepMimic shows that PPO with a pose-matching reward and reference-state initialization reproduces flips and kicks. Its reward is the template that BeyondMimic and SuperMimic still use:

\[ r_t = 0.65\,r^p + 0.1\,r^v + 0.15\,r^e + 0.1\,r^c, \qquad r^p = \exp\Big[-2\textstyle\sum_j \|\hat q_j \ominus q_j\|^2\Big] \]

AMP replaces hand-made tracking terms with an adversarial style reward. NPMP distills many experts into one latent motor primitive. SuperTrack (2021) is the first to train a tracker through a learned world model with BPTT, but on animated characters only.

Peng et al. 2018 [11] · Merel et al. 2019 [12] · Peng et al. 2021 AMP [13] · Fussell et al. 2021 SuperTrack [14]
2018–2022

Latent imagination and sim-to-real legged robots

On the model-based side, World Models, PlaNet, Dreamer and MBPO train behaviors inside learned models. Dreamer back-propagates λ-returns through latent imagination, close in spirit to SuperMimic. On the robotics side, domain randomization and massively parallel GPU simulation (Isaac Gym, legged_gym) make PPO-trained legged controllers transfer to hardware.

Tobin 2017 [15] · Peng 2018 dyn. rand. [16] · Ha & Schmidhuber 2018 [17] · Janner 2019 MBPO [18] · Hwangbo 2019 [19] · Hafner 2020 Dreamer [20] · Makoviychuk 2021 [21] · Rudin 2022 [24]
2022–2025

Differentiable simulation meets actor-critic

SHAC differentiates through a parallel differentiable simulator over short windows with a critic at the end, which is exactly the shape of SuperMimic's Eq. 2–3. Suh et al. show the catch: through stiff contacts, analytic gradients can be biased or have high variance. That motivates learning a smooth surrogate instead. ControlVAE, MoConVQ and SuperDyno scale learned-dynamics character control. Song et al. train real quadrupeds with differentiable simulation, and RWM trains real robots with PPO in a learned neural simulator.

\[ \mathcal L_{\text{SHAC}}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{Nh}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big[\sum_{t=t_0}^{t_0+h-1}\gamma^{t-t_0} r(s_t^i,a_t^i) + \gamma^{h} V_\phi(s_{t_0+h}^i)\Big] \]
Xu 2022 SHAC [22] · Suh 2022 [23] · Yao 2022 ControlVAE [25] · Hansen 2022 TD-MPC [26] · Wu 2023 DayDreamer [27] · Yao 2024 MoConVQ [28] · Lei 2025 SuperDyno [29] · Song 2025 [31] · Li 2025 RWM [32] · Hafner 2025 DreamerV3 [33]
2023–2026

Humanoid whole-body tracking at scale

PHC tracks almost all of AMASS in simulation. H2O/OmniH2O, ExBody, HumanPlus and ASAP bring mocap tracking to real humanoids, usually as a PPO teacher with privileged state distilled (DAgger) into a deployable student:

\[ \min_{\phi}\; \mathbb E_{s\sim d^{\pi_\phi}}\big\|\pi_\phi(o) - \pi^{\text{teacher}}(s)\big\|^2 \]

BeyondMimic (same first author as SuperMimic) gives a clean, transferable PPO tracking recipe plus guided diffusion for tasks. SONIC scales a single PPO tracker to about 600 hours of motion on a 128-GPU cluster. The common bottleneck is compute.

Luo 2023 PHC [34] · He 2024 H2O/OmniH2O [35,36] · Cheng 2024 ExBody [37] · Fu 2024 HumanPlus [38] · Luo 2024 PULSE [39] · He 2025 ASAP [40] · Liao 2026 BeyondMimic [30] · Luo 2026 SONIC [41]
Oct 2026

SuperMimic

SuperMimic combines both lines: the SuperTrack/SHAC idea of BPTT through a learned model with a critic, applied to the BeyondMimic/SONIC humanoid tracking problem, with domain-randomization context \(\xi\) and sensor noise handled explicitly so the result deploys on a real G1. One world model shared across all 129,514 clips, trained online, cuts compute by more than three orders of magnitude.

Liao, Shao, Long, Zakka, Horowitz, Abbeel, Sreenath 2026 [42]

6 · Open problemsWhat the paper leaves open

Model fidelity is the ceiling. The policy optimizes the model's return, not the simulator's. A deterministic model cannot represent state jumps, so BeyondMimic's random root-velocity pushes were removed from training, and push robustness comes only from the critic.

Scope. Training is flat ground only, with no stairs, objects, or terrain. About 12,700 of the 142,220 BONES-SEED clips were filtered out because they involve stairs, boxes or chairs, or have retargeting artifacts. Extending the model to scene contact is listed as ongoing work.

Simulator-only data. The model never sees real robot transitions. The authors suggest real data could refine it and that its differentiability could support system identification, which would bring it closer to DayDreamer-style learning on hardware.

Fair scaling comparisons. The headline ratio mixes GPU types and dataset sizes, and the benchmark motions are training motions, so failure counts measure coverage, not generalization. The controlled LAFAN1 comparison is the cleanest evidence.

Long horizons. Beyond H = 64, BPTT gets slower and worse. Longer-horizon credit assignment is still the critic's job.

Scaling laws. The paper's main promise is that one-GPU runs make it affordable to measure scaling laws for motion tracking (data hours × model size × return). That study hasn't been done yet.
